Use homepage, hub, category, and contextual links to help crawlers discover important deep pages without flattening the whole site.

For webmasters, the practical goal is simple: Give important deep pages a crawlable path that matches their business and editorial value.

## The Operating Rule

Deep pages do not all need homepage links, but important deep pages need reachable, relevant paths. A discovery workflow maps how authority and crawler access move from the homepage through hubs, categories, related content, and supporting pages.

## Technical Signals To Review

- Priority pages are reachable through crawlable HTML links, not only search boxes, scripts, or sitemaps.
- The link path makes topical sense and helps users, not just crawlers.
- Crawl depth and orphan reports identify pages whose discovery support does not match their value.
- Internal link updates are recorded with the URL cohort and follow-up date.

## Implementation And Audit Table

| Step | Control | Evidence | Implementation Decision |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Map priority pages | Revenue, content, product, or campaign importance | Do not optimize link paths for low-value pages first. |
| 2 | Trace click paths | Homepage, hub, category, and related-content routes | Find important pages buried too deeply or orphaned. |
| 3 | Add relevant links | Anchor context and source page quality | Use links that help users understand why the page matters. |
| 4 | Check technical access | Rendered links, robots, canonical, and status code | Confirm crawlers can follow the path. |
| 5 | Monitor evidence | Crawl depth, inspection status, and page cohort notes | Decide whether more architecture work is needed. |

Apply the rule consistently at template or system level. A clean implementation should make the intended page state obvious to users, crawlers, sitemaps, internal links, and reporting tools.

## Practical Scenario

A SaaS site has feature pages four clicks from the homepage and absent from related blog posts. The team adds a feature hub, contextual links from comparison articles, and sitemap cleanup. The deep pages now have multiple crawlable paths before entering the submission queue.

## Failure Modes To Avoid

- Adding footer links to every page instead of designing relevant discovery paths.
- Assuming sitemap inclusion compensates for orphaned important pages.
- Linking to redirected or noncanonical versions of deep URLs.
- Ignoring pages that are reachable only after client-side filters or search forms.

## Where FreeIndexer Fits

FreeIndexer should not replace internal linking. Use it after the site creates a real discovery path for the deep URL cohort.

## Turn The Findings Into An Action Queue

A diagnostic result is useful only when it changes what the team does next. Move each URL into one of four clear queues:

- **Ready:** the URL is useful, canonical, public, technically accessible, and ready for submission or normal monitoring.
- **Fix:** the URL has a correctable technical, content, linking, rendering, or reporting problem with an assigned owner.
- **Exclude:** the URL is intentionally redirected, noindexed, removed, duplicate, private, or otherwise outside the indexing target set.
- **Escalate:** the issue affects infrastructure, templates, migrations, security controls, or a large URL cohort and needs engineering or product input.

For this topic, the release rule is: **Give important deep pages a crawlable path that matches their business and editorial value**. Do not leave a URL in a vague pending state. Give it an owner, one next action, and a review date based on the evidence available.

## Evidence Log To Keep

| Field | What To Record | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Canonical URL | The final normalized URL checked by the operator | Prevents variants and redirects from splitting the investigation. |
| Cohort | Page type, template, campaign, locale, or backlink group | Reveals whether the issue is isolated or systemic. |
| Evidence source | Live response, URL Inspection, crawl, log, sitemap, or provider record | Makes the conclusion reproducible. |
| Change made | The exact technical, content, link, or workflow update | Separates action from assumption. |
| Owner and review date | Who is responsible and when the URL will be checked again | Stops the queue from becoming passive reporting. |

Keep submission dates in their own field. A submitted URL has completed an operational step; it has not automatically completed crawling, indexation, ranking, traffic, or conversion milestones. That separation makes the report more accurate and makes failed outcomes easier to diagnose.

## FAQ

### Does every deep page need a homepage link?

No. Use hubs and relevant contextual links so the link path matches importance and topic.

### How deep is too deep?

There is no universal number. Compare depth against the page's value and the site's crawl patterns.

### Where does FreeIndexer fit?

After link paths are improved, FreeIndexer can submit the priority deep URLs and track review dates.
