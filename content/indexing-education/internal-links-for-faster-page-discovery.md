Use internal links to help important pages get discovered faster, with practical placement rules for hubs, related pages, navigation, and fresh content.

For content teams, the practical goal is simple: Add useful internal links from relevant indexed pages before relying on external submission workflows.

## Quick Answer

Internal links are one of the cleanest discovery paths because they connect a new or updated page to the site structure Google already crawls. The goal is not to stuff links everywhere; it is to place relevant, crawlable links where users and crawlers naturally expect them.

## Signals That Matter

- The new page has at least one crawlable link from a relevant indexed page.
- Important pages are linked from hubs, categories, navigation, breadcrumbs, or related resources.
- Links use normal HTML anchors that can be discovered without fragile interactions.
- Anchor text is descriptive enough to explain the relationship without becoming spammy.

## Step-By-Step Workflow

| Step | Check | Evidence To Capture | Next Action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Choose source pages | Relevant hubs, category pages, and high-visibility pages | Link from pages that already belong to the same topic or user journey. |
| 2 | Place links naturally | Body sections, related resources, breadcrumbs, or modules | Use placements that help users, not hidden crawler-only links. |
| 3 | Check crawlability | HTML anchor, final URL, status code, and canonical | Avoid JavaScript-only or blocked discovery paths. |
| 4 | Refresh supporting pages | Update date and changed link modules | Make the link visible on the live version. |
| 5 | Track discovery | Inspection result, crawl date, and referral path | Record which internal link helped the URL enter the queue. |

A useful tracker keeps the evidence and the conclusion separate. Record what the URL returned, what the tool reported, what changed, who owns the next action, and when the page should be reviewed again.

## Worked Example

A new comparison page is in the sitemap but has no internal links. Instead of submitting it repeatedly, the team links it from the comparison hub, a relevant product guide, and two related articles. The page now has context, user value, and a stronger discovery path before FreeIndexer submission.

The point of the example is not the exact numbers. It is the sequence: verify the real page, classify the issue, make one defensible change, and preserve enough evidence to evaluate the result later.

## Common Mistakes

- Relying on XML sitemaps while leaving important pages orphaned.
- Adding links from unrelated pages just to increase link count.
- Using buttons or scripts that do not expose crawlable anchor links.
- Forgetting to update internal links after a slug or canonical change.

## Where FreeIndexer Fits

## Turn The Findings Into An Action Queue

A diagnostic result is useful only when it changes what the team does next. Move each URL into one of four clear queues:

- **Ready:** the URL is useful, canonical, public, technically accessible, and ready for submission or normal monitoring.
- **Fix:** the URL has a correctable technical, content, linking, rendering, or reporting problem with an assigned owner.
- **Exclude:** the URL is intentionally redirected, noindexed, removed, duplicate, private, or otherwise outside the indexing target set.
- **Escalate:** the issue affects infrastructure, templates, migrations, security controls, or a large URL cohort and needs engineering or product input.

For this topic, the release rule is: **Add useful internal links from relevant indexed pages before relying on external submission workflows**. Do not leave a URL in a vague pending state. Give it an owner, one next action, and a review date based on the evidence available.

## Evidence Log To Keep

| Field | What To Record | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Canonical URL | The final normalized URL checked by the operator | Prevents variants and redirects from splitting the investigation. |
| Cohort | Page type, template, campaign, locale, or backlink group | Reveals whether the issue is isolated or systemic. |
| Evidence source | Live response, URL Inspection, crawl, log, sitemap, or provider record | Makes the conclusion reproducible. |
| Change made | The exact technical, content, link, or workflow update | Separates action from assumption. |
| Owner and review date | Who is responsible and when the URL will be checked again | Stops the queue from becoming passive reporting. |

Keep submission dates in their own field. A submitted URL has completed an operational step; it has not automatically completed crawling, indexation, ranking, traffic, or conversion milestones. That separation makes the report more accurate and makes failed outcomes easier to diagnose.

## FAQ

### How many internal links does a new page need?

Enough to make the page reachable and contextually clear. Quality and relevance matter more than a fixed count.

### Should every new post link from the homepage?

Not always. Use the homepage for timely or strategic pages; use hubs and related articles for topical discovery.

### Where does FreeIndexer fit?

Use FreeIndexer after links are in place so submission supports a page that is already discoverable within the site.
