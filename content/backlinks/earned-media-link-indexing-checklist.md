Use an earned media link indexing checklist to verify live coverage, link visibility, canonical state, access, and follow-up reporting.

For pr teams, the practical goal is simple: Verify earned coverage like an SEO asset before submitting or including it in link reports.

## Quick Answer

Earned media links often matter more than bulk-created URLs, so they deserve careful verification. The checklist confirms the article is live, the link is visible, the page is accessible, and the report separates discovery work from outcome claims.

## Signals That Matter

- The media page is public, stable, and not blocked behind paywall or login conditions that prevent normal crawling.
- The brand mention or backlink is visible in the rendered article and points to the intended destination.
- Canonical, noindex, and redirect signals do not undermine the page's discoverability.
- Reporting includes evidence date, placement type, target URL, and follow-up status.

## Step-By-Step Workflow

| Step | Check | Evidence To Capture | Next Action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm coverage | Live URL, publisher, article title, and publish date | Make sure the placement exists before SEO tracking begins. |
| 2 | Verify link | Anchor, target URL, rendered visibility, and rel attributes | Record the exact asset earned. |
| 3 | Check access signals | Status, robots, noindex, canonical, and paywall behavior | Understand whether crawlers can process the page. |
| 4 | Classify priority | Publisher quality, relevance, audience, and target page value | Submit the highest-value links first. |
| 5 | Report follow-up | Submission date, review date, and evidence notes | Avoid promising Google-side outcomes. |

A useful tracker keeps the evidence and the conclusion separate. Record what the URL returned, what the tool reported, what changed, who owns the next action, and when the page should be reviewed again.

## Worked Example

A startup earns coverage in nine industry publications. The checklist finds one nofollow mention without a link, one article behind a login wall, and seven visible crawlable links. The SEO team submits the seven verified URLs and reports all nine placements accurately.

The point of the example is not the exact numbers. It is the sequence: verify the real page, classify the issue, make one defensible change, and preserve enough evidence to evaluate the result later.

## Common Mistakes

- Counting brand mentions as backlinks without checking the rendered page.
- Submitting URLs hidden behind access controls without noting the limitation.
- Ignoring canonical tags on syndicated versions of earned coverage.
- Promising that a high-quality publication will automatically appear in link reports.

## Turn The Findings Into An Action Queue

A diagnostic result is useful only when it changes what the team does next. Move each URL into one of four clear queues:

- **Ready:** the URL is useful, canonical, public, technically accessible, and ready for submission or normal monitoring.
- **Fix:** the URL has a correctable technical, content, linking, rendering, or reporting problem with an assigned owner.
- **Exclude:** the URL is intentionally redirected, noindexed, removed, duplicate, private, or otherwise outside the indexing target set.
- **Escalate:** the issue affects infrastructure, templates, migrations, security controls, or a large URL cohort and needs engineering or product input.

For this topic, the release rule is: **Verify earned coverage like an SEO asset before submitting or including it in link reports**. Do not leave a URL in a vague pending state. Give it an owner, one next action, and a review date based on the evidence available.

## Evidence Log To Keep

| Field | What To Record | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Canonical URL | The final normalized URL checked by the operator | Prevents variants and redirects from splitting the investigation. |
| Cohort | Page type, template, campaign, locale, or backlink group | Reveals whether the issue is isolated or systemic. |
| Evidence source | Live response, URL Inspection, crawl, log, sitemap, or provider record | Makes the conclusion reproducible. |
| Change made | The exact technical, content, link, or workflow update | Separates action from assumption. |
| Owner and review date | Who is responsible and when the URL will be checked again | Stops the queue from becoming passive reporting. |

Keep submission dates in their own field. A submitted URL has completed an operational step; it has not automatically completed crawling, indexation, ranking, traffic, or conversion milestones. That separation makes the report more accurate and makes failed outcomes easier to diagnose.

## FAQ

### Should nofollow earned links be tracked?

They can be tracked as PR evidence, but report rel attributes and avoid overclaiming SEO outcomes.

### What if the article is behind a paywall?

Record the access condition and decide whether it is worth submission or only PR reporting.
