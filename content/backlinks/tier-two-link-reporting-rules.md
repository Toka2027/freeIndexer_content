Set tier two link reporting rules that separate verification, submission, discovery evidence, risk notes, and realistic client language.

For seo agencys, the practical goal is simple: Keep tiered link reports evidence-based and conservative so the workflow does not turn into claim inflation.

## Quick Answer

Tier two link work can create reporting pressure because volume looks impressive. Good reporting separates what was verified, what was submitted, what evidence changed, what carries risk, and what cannot be promised.

## Signals That Matter

- The report distinguishes linking-page verification from submission and Google-side outcomes.
- Low-quality, irrelevant, or risky link sources are flagged instead of hidden inside volume totals.
- Campaign notes preserve vendor, target, anchor, placement type, and evidence date.
- Client language avoids guarantees, ranking promises, or claims beyond the evidence.

## Step-By-Step Workflow

| Step | Check | Evidence To Capture | Next Action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define allowed sources | Quality threshold, relevance, and risk criteria | Prevent risky URLs from entering the workflow. |
| 2 | Verify rows | Live URL, target, anchor, access, and context | Do not submit unverified link lists. |
| 3 | Record submission | Tool, date, owner, and campaign | Separate action from outcome. |
| 4 | Track evidence | Crawl, index, link report, or discovery notes | Report what changed and what is still unknown. |
| 5 | Review language | Client claims, caveats, and exclusions | Keep reporting accurate and defensible. |

A useful tracker keeps the evidence and the conclusion separate. Record what the URL returned, what the tool reported, what changed, who owns the next action, and when the page should be reviewed again.

## Worked Example

A link team delivers 1,500 tier two URLs. The reporting rules exclude low-quality sources, tag unverified placements, and report only 640 verified submissions with risk notes. The client gets a cleaner, less inflated update.

The point of the example is not the exact numbers. It is the sequence: verify the real page, classify the issue, make one defensible change, and preserve enough evidence to evaluate the result later.

## Common Mistakes

- Reporting raw tier-two volume as if it equals indexed links.
- Hiding risky source types in aggregate numbers.
- Submitting unverified rows from provider exports.
- Promising ranking movement from tiered link activity.

## Turn The Findings Into An Action Queue

A diagnostic result is useful only when it changes what the team does next. Move each URL into one of four clear queues:

- **Ready:** the URL is useful, canonical, public, technically accessible, and ready for submission or normal monitoring.
- **Fix:** the URL has a correctable technical, content, linking, rendering, or reporting problem with an assigned owner.
- **Exclude:** the URL is intentionally redirected, noindexed, removed, duplicate, private, or otherwise outside the indexing target set.
- **Escalate:** the issue affects infrastructure, templates, migrations, security controls, or a large URL cohort and needs engineering or product input.

For this topic, the release rule is: **Keep tiered link reports evidence-based and conservative so the workflow does not turn into claim inflation**. Do not leave a URL in a vague pending state. Give it an owner, one next action, and a review date based on the evidence available.

## Evidence Log To Keep

| Field | What To Record | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Canonical URL | The final normalized URL checked by the operator | Prevents variants and redirects from splitting the investigation. |
| Cohort | Page type, template, campaign, locale, or backlink group | Reveals whether the issue is isolated or systemic. |
| Evidence source | Live response, URL Inspection, crawl, log, sitemap, or provider record | Makes the conclusion reproducible. |
| Change made | The exact technical, content, link, or workflow update | Separates action from assumption. |
| Owner and review date | Who is responsible and when the URL will be checked again | Stops the queue from becoming passive reporting. |

Keep submission dates in their own field. A submitted URL has completed an operational step; it has not automatically completed crawling, indexation, ranking, traffic, or conversion milestones. That separation makes the report more accurate and makes failed outcomes easier to diagnose.

## FAQ

### Should tier two links be in client reports?

Only with careful context, evidence fields, and realistic language.

### Can FreeIndexer verify link quality?

No. Operators should verify quality and access before adding URLs to FreeIndexer.

### What language is safest?

Report verified actions and observed evidence, not promises of indexing, rankings, or traffic.
