Review AI-generated pages for usefulness, originality, evidence, internal links, and technical readiness before they enter an indexing queue.

For content teams, the practical goal is simple: Require human editorial and technical QA before AI-assisted pages are submitted for discovery.

## Quick Answer

AI-generated or AI-assisted pages should pass the same usefulness and technical checks as any other page, with extra attention to originality, evidence, intent fit, and editorial ownership. The risk is not that a tool helped create the draft; the risk is publishing scaled pages that add little value.

## Signals That Matter

- The page answers a real user need with specific, accurate, and verifiable information.
- The content includes original examples, product context, data, screenshots, workflows, or expert review where appropriate.
- The page avoids scaled duplication, doorway patterns, unsupported claims, and generic filler.
- Technical basics such as canonicals, links, sitemap inclusion, and metadata are clean.

## Step-By-Step Workflow

| Step | Check | Evidence To Capture | Next Action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Check intent fit | Query purpose, audience, and page promise | Revise pages that do not clearly satisfy the search task. |
| 2 | Add original value | Examples, product details, field notes, data, or screenshots | Move beyond generic generated explanations. |
| 3 | Review accuracy | Claims, sources, dates, and product workflow details | Remove unsupported or misleading statements. |
| 4 | Run technical QA | Title, meta, canonical, internal links, noindex, and rendering | Make the page ready before submission. |
| 5 | Track ownership | Editor, reviewer, update date, and submission reason | Keep accountability attached to the URL. |

A useful tracker keeps the evidence and the conclusion separate. Record what the URL returned, what the tool reported, what changed, who owns the next action, and when the page should be reviewed again.

## Worked Example

A team drafts 200 location pages with AI assistance. Instead of submitting all of them, they require local proof, unique service details, useful FAQs, internal links, and editorial review. Pages without real local value are consolidated or held back.

The point of the example is not the exact numbers. It is the sequence: verify the real page, classify the issue, make one defensible change, and preserve enough evidence to evaluate the result later.

## Common Mistakes

- Publishing AI-assisted pages without adding specific evidence or examples.
- Using unique words but not unique value.
- Submitting scaled low-value pages and calling the issue an indexing delay.
- Skipping human review because the metadata looks complete.

## Where FreeIndexer Fits

FreeIndexer should receive only AI-assisted URLs that pass editorial QA. Campaign notes should identify the review owner and the reason the page is ready.

## Turn The Findings Into An Action Queue

A diagnostic result is useful only when it changes what the team does next. Move each URL into one of four clear queues:

- **Ready:** the URL is useful, canonical, public, technically accessible, and ready for submission or normal monitoring.
- **Fix:** the URL has a correctable technical, content, linking, rendering, or reporting problem with an assigned owner.
- **Exclude:** the URL is intentionally redirected, noindexed, removed, duplicate, private, or otherwise outside the indexing target set.
- **Escalate:** the issue affects infrastructure, templates, migrations, security controls, or a large URL cohort and needs engineering or product input.

For this topic, the release rule is: **Require human editorial and technical QA before AI-assisted pages are submitted for discovery**. Do not leave a URL in a vague pending state. Give it an owner, one next action, and a review date based on the evidence available.

## Evidence Log To Keep

| Field | What To Record | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Canonical URL | The final normalized URL checked by the operator | Prevents variants and redirects from splitting the investigation. |
| Cohort | Page type, template, campaign, locale, or backlink group | Reveals whether the issue is isolated or systemic. |
| Evidence source | Live response, URL Inspection, crawl, log, sitemap, or provider record | Makes the conclusion reproducible. |
| Change made | The exact technical, content, link, or workflow update | Separates action from assumption. |
| Owner and review date | Who is responsible and when the URL will be checked again | Stops the queue from becoming passive reporting. |

Keep submission dates in their own field. A submitted URL has completed an operational step; it has not automatically completed crawling, indexation, ranking, traffic, or conversion milestones. That separation makes the report more accurate and makes failed outcomes easier to diagnose.

## FAQ

### Can AI-assisted content be indexed?

The practical question is whether the page is useful, original, accurate, and technically accessible.

### Should AI pages have special metadata?

No special label is required for indexing workflows, but internal QA notes and ownership are helpful.

### Can FreeIndexer submit AI-assisted pages?

Yes, if the pages pass editorial and technical QA like any other ready URL.
